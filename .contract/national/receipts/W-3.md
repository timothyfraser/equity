> Does this unevenness extend beyond New York? We computed the same indices for 224 MPOs nationwide (Figure 8). The median MPO scores 0.915 on race and 0.957 on income. On race, 199 of 224 MPOs score below 0.95, and 79 score below 0.90; on income, 78 score below 0.95, and 9 below 0.90. >
> MPOs with more on-road PM2.5 emissions per resident spread that burden less evenly by race (Figure 8, right column). Across 224 MPOs, each one-unit increase in log emissions per resident is associated with a change in the race index of β = -0.049 (95% CI: -0.065 to -0.034; p < 0.001; R² = 0.149). The income slope is smaller, and its interval nearly reaches zero (β = -0.010; 95% CI: -0.019 to -0.0003; p = 0.044; R² = 0.018).
>
> These gaps are not what chance alone would produce. 222 of 224 MPOs are significantly more uneven in emissions burden by race than we would expect by chance (p0 < 0.05), as are 224 of 224 on income. Chance alone would give much higher scores: shuffling race labels among residents of Virginia's Staunton-Augusta-Waynesboro MPO gives an expected race index of 0.959, against 0.708 observed. For the median MPO, sampling error moves its index within a 95% range of 0.060 on race, 0.019 on income, 0.019 on education and 0.013 on age.
